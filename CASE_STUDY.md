# Case Study: From a One-Month Stopgap to a Group-Wide ERP

## Background

Lira Group of Industries operates multiple companies spanning polymer manufacturing, packaging, distribution, and warehousing. When I joined as Head of IT & ERP in 2023, the group's previous business software had failed, and the companies needed a working system quickly to keep sales, stock and receivables moving.

## Phase one: a working system in one month

I scoped the minimum the business needed to keep running — Sales, Inventory and Accounts Receivable — and took it live within one month. I did the requirement analysis, scope of work, blueprint, reports and server deployment myself. Freelance Java developers coded it to my design.

The plan was for this to be a temporary solution while the group chose a larger third-party ERP.

## Why it stayed

Users adopted the system well. Instead of buying a new ERP, management asked me to keep extending it, part by part.

## Seven phases of growth

Over 7 phases I grew the starter system into a multi-company ERP that now serves 8 group companies, adding Administration, Field Force Management, Shipment Management, Procurement, Accounts Payable, MIS dashboards and Tally integration along the way.

Two design choices made that growth possible:

- **Data-driven configuration.** Screens, menus, access rules, and business rules live as configuration, scoped per business, so adding a company is a configuration task rather than a new development cycle.
- **Design before code, every phase.** For each phase I wrote the blueprint first — functional design, data model, screen design and security — so the developers built to a specification, not a guess.

## Delivery

I ran requirement discovery with sales, accounts, and factory/warehouse teams, reviewed every deliverable against the blueprint, owned testing and UAT, coordinated master-data and opening-balance preparation with each department, ran staff training, and managed each phase's go-live. Since 2026 I also deliver enhancements through AI-assisted development under strict change control.

## Result

- **8 companies** run on one platform — manufacturing, packaging, distribution, and warehousing units alike
- **150+ reports** for sales, purchase, inventory and finance
- New business units are onboarded through configuration

## What I'd highlight

The interesting part isn't "an ERP was built". It's that a system delivered in one month under pressure was designed cleanly enough to grow, phase by phase, into the group's ERP — instead of being thrown away.
